# Grand Design RV customer lifecycle personas

## One evolving customer relationship

**Grand Design isn't managing three separate audiences. It's managing one evolving customer relationship.**

**SHOP → OWN → UPGRADE → OWN → UPGRADE**

That becomes especially interesting because Grand Design has expanded beyond its historic towable portfolio into motorized RVs. Someone's next Grand Design doesn't necessarily have to look like their current Grand Design.

|  | **Shopper** | **Owner** | **Lifecycle / Upgrader** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mindset** | “Which RV is right for me?” | “Help me get the most from the RV I own.” | “What should I move into next?” |
| **Primary goal** | Build confidence in a high-consideration purchase | Maximize enjoyment, reliability and ownership value | Find a better fit as needs and experience change |
| **Key needs** | Understand models and floorplans; compare features; see real inventory; understand pricing; find a trusted dealer | Manuals and how-to content; warranty/service; maintenance; parts/accessories; troubleshooting; owner education | Compare current RV with potential upgrade; understand new features/models; assess trade-in; locate inventory; reconnect with dealer |
| **Typical experiences** | Inspiration → model discovery → comparison → floorplan → inventory → dealer/quote | Registration → onboarding → how-to → maintenance → service/support → community | New need → research → comparison → trade-in → dealer → purchase |
| **Content that matters** | Product imagery/video, floorplans, specs, comparisons, lifestyle content, buying guides | Manuals, videos, maintenance guides, FAQs, service information, accessories | “What’s new,” model comparisons, upgrade guides, personalized recommendations, trade-in content |
| **Important context/data** | Browsing behavior, models/floorplans viewed, location, inventory availability, dealer interactions | Owned model, model year, configuration, warranty, service history, preferences | Owned RV + previous behavior + emerging needs + dealer relationship |
| **Activation opportunity** | Turn anonymous interest into **qualified purchase intent** | Turn ownership into **engagement and loyalty** | Turn an existing relationship into **repeat purchase** |
| **Business outcome** | Leads, dealer visits, quote requests, sales | Satisfaction, retention, service engagement, advocacy | Trade-ins, upgrades, cross-sell, lifetime value |

## The digital-experience implication

This gives you a strong distinction between **content management** and **content activation**:

**Manage once**

- RV product information
- specifications and floorplans
- assets
- manuals
- lifestyle/editorial content
- service information

**Activate differently**

- To a **Shopper**, activate it for *discovery and consideration*.
- To an **Owner**, activate it for *ownership and support*.
- To an **Upgrader**, activate it using what you know about their existing relationship to drive *the next best experience*.

That last persona is where I see the most interesting opportunity. Instead of treating an existing owner who returns to GrandDesignRV.com as another anonymous shopper, Grand Design could potentially recognize the lifecycle context:

**“You own a Reflection 315RLTS. Here’s what has changed, what might fit your evolving needs, what local dealers have available, and how to take the next step.”**

## Naming the actual card

I would call the three personas **Shopper | Owner | Upgrader** on the actual card. “Lifecycle” describes the strategy, whereas **Upgrader** describes the person and their intent much more naturally.
